I spend most of my working days translating and writing Spanish iGaming content for casino and sports betting operators, so when a regulatory conference lands in Madrid, I pay attention. 

This year's Gaming in Spain conference took place on **15 October at the ECIJA auditorium**, and I wasn't in the room. 

What I have done, over the past couple of weeks, is work through the **published programme**, the **DGOJ's own summary**, and the **recap published by AZARplus**, along with coverage from **Gaming Intelligence**.

What caught my attention wasn't any single headline. It was how often the discussions connected two things I think about constantly in my own work: 

- regulation
- the experience operators offer their customers

A rule only means something once it shows up in an interface, an email or a landing page. That's the gap I spend my days working in, and it's the gap this conference kept circling back to.

So consider this my working notes. I'm not going to pretend I sat in the audience. I'm going to tell you what the coverage says, where the gaps are, and what questions I'd be asking if I were reviewing Spanish iGaming content for an operator targeting this market right now.

## What did Gaming in Spain 2026 put on the agenda?

The conference was organised by Gaming in Europe and led by **Willem van Oort**, held at the [ECIJA offices in Madrid](https://www.gamingin.eu/event-details/gisconference2026). 

The attendee list, based on the coverage, included the Dirección General de Ordenación del Juego (DGOJ) alongside operators, legal advisers from firms like ECIJA and LOYRA Abogados, and technology and consulting representatives.

The reported themes were fairly consistent across sources. Mikel Arana, the DGOJ's Director General, opened with an analysis of the regulatory calendar, made more pressing by Spain's snap general election, moved up to 29 November. 

From there, the day moved through joint deposit limits, player protection measures, the ongoing assessment of advertising rules, and **competition from unlicensed gambling operators** (a topic that came up again and again).

I want to flag something before going further: this is a conference recap, not a formal regulatory text. 

Where I mention figures or specific findings below, I've tried to be clear about whether they come from a presentation summary or a fully published report. Those are two different things.

## How did the conference frame advertising rules for Spain's gambling operators?

Advertising wasn't treated as a standalone topic at Gaming in Spain 2026. It came up as part of a broader conversation about what sustainable growth looks like for licensed operators in Spain.

### What did the DGOJ present about commercial communications?

According to the [DGOJ's own event summary](https://www.ordenacionjuego.es/novedades/dgoj-participa-gaming-spain-conference-2026-abordar-retos-regulatorios-juego-online), Mikel Arana presented preliminary findings from the **impact assessment of Royal Decree 958/2020**, Spain's regulation on gambling commercial communications. 

The DGOJ states that the full report will be published publicly "in the coming weeks" (meaning what Arana shared at the conference was a summary, not the complete document).

Arana also offered a pointed reflection on the sector's growth model, quoted in the AZARplus recap: **"The real question is whether it's acceptable for the sector's future growth to depend on a permanent intensification of acquisition and spending, especially among the most vulnerable groups."** 

That's a direct challenge to any campaign strategy built purely on volume.

I'd treat any specific percentage or comparison figure from this presentation carefully until the full report is out. A single slide at a conference rarely tells you the methodology, the comparison period or the scope of what was measured (and without that context, repeating a number in Spanish iGaming content risks misrepresenting it).

### What would I take from that as a content writer?

Here's where my own perspective comes in, separate from what was reported. 

When I review an ad or a promotional email for the Spanish market, I'm not thinking about whether the law was technically followed. I'm thinking about what a player actually understands after reading it. That means looking at: 

- the headline
- the offer terms
- the activation instructions as one unit, not three separate pieces written by three different teams.

A headline promising a bonus means something different depending on whether the qualifying conditions are two lines down or buried in a linked terms page. 

I'd also want the warning messages mentioned in the DGOJ's presentation **to sit naturally within the copy, not bolted on as an afterthought that nobody reads**.

To be clear, I'm not suggesting the conference proved that any particular writing approach improves conversion or compliance outcomes. Nobody presented that data, and I wouldn't claim it. 

What I'm describing is a professional habit: when regulation tightens around how an offer gets communicated, the practical fix usually isn't a legal disclaimer. It's making **the whole message easier to follow** in the first place.

## Why are deposit limits bringing the player journey into focus?

This was, by most accounts, the most technically detailed part of the day.

### What did the conference discussion cover?

The new joint deposit limit system was approved by **Spain's Council of Ministers on 23 June**. 

For the first time, it sets a single global deposit limit across every online gambling operator where a person holds an account, rather than each operator managing its own separate limit. 

The DGOJ will manage the system.

Justo Sancho, the DGOJ's Director of the Safe Gaming Control Division, spoke about the technical work this requires (specifically, deploying cloud-native infrastructure capable of keeping the supporting APIs running continuously, so limits stay consistent across operators in real time). 

Consultant Sander Hoogendoorn offered a counterpoint, [according to AZARplus](https://www.azarplus.com/gaming-in-spain-reune-al-sector-en-una-jornada-de-debate-analisis-y-reivindicacion-ante-el-nuevo-escenario-regulatorio/), warning that **an overly rigid rollout could create operational friction and, in a worst case, push some users toward unlicensed platforms instead**.

I'll say plainly that I don't have a confirmed implementation date for operators from the sources I reviewed. 

Approval by the Council of Ministers, DGOJ technical rollout, and the date operators must actually have the system live are three separate milestones, and I'd want to see each one dated individually before writing anything definitive for a client.

### Which Spanish messages would I want to review?

This is squarely in my territory. 

A joint deposit limit touches customer support copy, help centre articles, in-app messaging and account notifications — basically every point where a player interacts with their own money.

Here's a hypothetical, purely to illustrate the kind of gap I'd be checking for: imagine a player tries to deposit €50, but their cross-operator limit has already been reached on a different platform. 

What does the interface tell them? Does it explain why the deposit failed, or just that it failed? Does it point them toward a next step (checking their limit, contacting support, waiting until the next period resets)?

I'd want the explanation of **how the limit works, the in-the-moment error message, and any promotional email about deposit limits to use the same terminology**. 

It sounds like a small thing. In practice, inconsistent wording between a help page and an app notification is one of the most common gaps I find when auditing Spanish iGaming services for operators.

## How is the industry responding to competition from unlicensed sites?

Unlicensed gambling came up repeatedly, and it was framed less as a side issue and more as the backdrop to everything else discussed that day.

Jorge Hinojosa, Director General of Jdigital, the Spanish gambling trade association, argued for a **long-term regulatory approach** (one stable enough to hold for ten or fifteen years). 

He also clarified that Jdigital hadn't opposed payment method ownership verification, correcting how Arana had characterized the association's position earlier in the day. 

Jdigital's concern, Hinojosa said, was about how that obligation gets applied in practice. 

He called **unauthorized gambling "the real elephant in the room"** and announced a white paper planned for December, built around the slogan "evaluate before legislating."

That's the [reported position](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/es/marketing/75923-melilla-gaming-in-spain/). 

My own view, as someone who writes for licensed operators, is narrower and more practical: content can't solve a structural problem like unlicensed competition. 

What licensed operators can do through their Spanish iGaming content is **make the tangible advantages of being licensed easy to find** (things like verifiable customer support channels, clear account and withdrawal processes, and visible responsible gambling tools). 

I wouldn't claim that clearer copy stops someone from using an unlicensed site. That's not a claim the conference supports, and it's not one I'd make on my own authority either.

## What's the issue with registration, KYC and abandonment?

Gmonitor's session looked at the Spanish onboarding journey:

- registration
- identity verification
- where users drop off

This connects directly to something I care about: **consistent terminology through a sign-up flow**. 

But I'd also flag that copy is only one variable here. 

Abandonment during KYC is often shaped by document requirements, verification turnaround time and interface design, not just the words on the page. 

I wouldn't want to overstate what better copy alone can fix in that funnel.

## What I'd review in your Spanish iGaming content after this coverage

If you're a marketer working with operators targeting Spain, here's what I'd put on a review list based on everything above:

- **Check promotional claims against the current Spanish offer**. Terms change, and a headline that was accurate three months ago might not be now.
- **Review deposit and verification messages in the live interface, not just in the copy doc**. What looks fine in a Word file can read very differently once it's sitting inside an app notification.
- **Compare terminology across ads, landing pages, emails and help content**. Pick one term for a deposit limit, a verification step or a bonus condition, and use it everywhere.
- **Raise unresolved questions with the operator's product or compliance team rather than guessing**. If you're not sure how a limit interacts with a specific account type, ask before you write around it.
- **Record which pieces of content need updating** once the joint deposit limit system and the full advertising impact report are formally in place, so nothing gets missed when the rules shift.

Each of these sits within what I'd consider a writer and translator's role: making sure the language matches the actual product and process, and flagging anything that needs a legal or compliance sign-off rather than trying to resolve it myself.

## Frequently asked questions

### What was discussed at the Gaming in Spain 2026 conference?

The conference covered regulatory change, the new joint deposit limit system, advertising rules for gambling operators, and competition from unlicensed gambling sites, with speakers from the DGOJ, legal firms, operators and trade associations.

### When will Spain's full advertising impact report be published?

The DGOJ has stated that the complete impact assessment of Royal Decree 958/2020, covering commercial communications for gambling activities, will be published publicly in the coming weeks following the conference. Only preliminary findings were presented at the event itself.

### What is Spain's joint deposit limit system?

Approved by the Council of Ministers on 23 June, it sets a single global deposit limit across every online gambling operator where a person holds an account, managed by the DGOJ, replacing the previous model where each operator set its own independent limit.

It raises practical questions for anyone writing or localizing content for Spanish iGaming markets, particularly around consistent terminology for deposit limits, clear promotional terms, and messaging that matches what players actually experience in the product.

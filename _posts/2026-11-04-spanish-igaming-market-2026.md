Here's a contrast that caught my eye this month: **bet365 topped Blask's Brand Accumulated Power ranking for Spain in the October 2026 snapshot, at 16.53%**, nearly double bwin's 8.41%. 

And yet, when you look at year-on-year growth among the top ten brands, bwin (67.22%) and Winamax (64.18%) posted the biggest gains, according to Blask data cited by European Gaming (europeangaming.eu). 

bet365's own brand interest actually fell 5.52% over the same period.

So who's "winning" in Spain right now? It depends which question you're asking. And that's really the point of this post.

## What the Latest Data Tells Us About Spain's iGaming Market

If you're planning your next batch of Spanish iGaming content, I'd ask a different question first: which pages already on your site could use that budget before you write anything new? 

As someone who writes and localizes copy for casino and sports betting operators targeting Spain, I spend more time auditing existing pages than drafting fresh ones. 

This post walks through **how I'd approach that audit**, using the current market data as context, not as a reason to chase trends.

### The most visible brand and the fastest growers were different

Blask's Brand Accumulated Power (BAP) metric measures a brand's share of total consumer interest in a market (essentially, how much of the "oxygen" a brand is taking up compared to its competitors). 

It's a snapshot of visibility, not revenue.

In the October 2026 data, **bet365 held the largest share of that visibility in Spain, [at 16.53%](https://europeangaming.eu/portal/spain-online-gambling-market-brands-trends/), with Sportium, Casino Barcelona, and Codere rounding out the top five**.

But BAP tells you where attention sits today. It doesn't tell you where it's heading.

That's where year-on-year growth comes in. 

Among the top ten brands by BAP, bwin and Winamax recorded the steepest increases: 67.22% and 64.18% respectively, while bet365's interest dipped slightly. 

Blask co-founder Dmitry Belianin offered one explanation to European Gaming: "Bet365 is not untouchable though; local challengers have been taking share."

I'd treat that as his professional read on the data, not a settled fact. 

But it's a useful prompt: **a brand's current size and its momentum are two different stories, and your content strategy probably needs to account for both**.

### What Spain's Q2 gambling revenue adds to the picture

Separately, Spain's Dirección General de Ordenación del Juego (DGOJ) reported that the **online gambling market generated €539.18 million in gross gaming revenue in Q2 2026**, up 31.16% year-on-year and [18.66% quarter-on-quarter](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/es/finanzas/75566-resultados-juego-espana/).

Betting was the main driver, growing 45% quarter-on-quarter, while casino grew a steadier 5.56%.

I want to be clear about what this figure is and isn't. 

It's an official market revenue number from the regulator, covering a different period (Q2 2026) than the Blask brand snapshot (October 2026). 

It tells you the market as a whole is growing. It doesn't tell you which pages on your site are working, which questions players are typing into Google right now, or where your content has gone stale. For that, you need to look at your own site and your own search data.

## How I'd Choose Which Spanish iGaming Pages to Review First

Before diving into a full site overhaul, you should prioritize your efforts to make the biggest impact fast. 

Understanding where to focus first can save time, boost performance, and help you deliver real value where it matters most. 

Here's how I'd approach tackling Spanish iGaming pages for review and refinement.

### Start with the searches that already bring people to your site

Before writing anything new, I open Google Search Console and filter by Spain. **I look at queries and pages together (not in isolation)** because a query without its matching page doesn't tell you much.

I'm specifically looking for searches that reveal a clear question. Something like "cómo verificar mi cuenta" is a gift: it tells you exactly what the player wants to know. 

Once I find a query like that, I open whatever page is currently ranking for it and time myself answering the question as a first-time reader would. 

[If it takes too long to find the answer](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en), that's a signal.

### Work out where the page falls short

From there, I try to sort pages into one of three buckets. I find this separation useful because each one points to a different fix:

| Situation | What I'd check | Likely fix |
|---|---|---|
| Page appears in search but rarely gets clicked | Which queries trigger it, its average position, whether the title tag actually promises an answer | Rewrite the title/meta, not necessarily the body |
| Page gets clicks, but instructions are outdated | Compare the copy against the operator's current Spanish site or app | Update steps, screenshots, terminology |
| Support keeps hearing the same question | Ask support for example tickets; check if any page answers it clearly, and where | Add the answer to an existing page, or build a new one |

This sorting step is where most of the actual judgment happens. 

It's tempting to jump straight to "let's write a new guide," but in my experience, **a good chunk of underperforming Spanish iGaming content just needs a more honest title tag or an updated screenshot, not a rewrite**.

## When I'd Update, Localise or Write New Spanish iGaming Content (3 Steps)

Once I know what's wrong with a page, I have to decide what kind of work it actually needs. I think of it as three separate decisions, not one.

### 1. Update the Spanish page, if the answer is already there

I keep **whatever still helps the reader, correct outdated steps, swap in current screenshots, and bring the terminology in line with what's in the app today**. 

Before I touch anything, I ask the operator for their current material. I don't want to "fix" a page based on my own assumptions about what changed.

### 2. Localise the English page, if the process differs in Spain

This is where Spanish iGaming localization earns its name. It's not the same exercise as translation. 

A verification flow, a deposit limit, or a promotional term **that works for a UK audience might not apply the same way under Spanish gambling laws**, or the product itself might behave differently for Spanish accounts. 

I verify which steps, options, and screen labels actually apply in Spain before I adapt a single sentence.

### 3. Write a new page, only if the question needs its own answer

My test here is simple: can an existing guide answer this clearly in a short section? 

If yes, I add it there instead of fragmenting your site with another thin page. 

If the question genuinely **needs a fuller explanation and has no natural home**, that's when I'd recommend a new piece of Spanish casino or sportsbook content.

## A Worked Example: How I'd Fix a Spanish Account Verification Guide

To make this concrete, let me walk through a hypothetical. 

(I'm making up the specifics here; this isn't a real client page, just a composite of the kind of issue I see often).

Say a guide ranks for "cómo verificar mi cuenta". It tells readers to select "Verificar perfil," but the current app actually says "Verificar cuenta". 

The guide also ends the moment the reader uploads a document, with no mention of what happens next.

### What I'd check with the operator

I'd ask for current screenshots of the Spanish verification flow: not English ones, not old ones. 

**I'd confirm exactly where the player starts, what the button is labeled, and what the player sees immediately after submitting their document**. 

If the product team hasn't confirmed a review timeframe, I leave that detail out entirely rather than guess.

### What I'd keep and what I'd rewrite

I'd keep the sections that still match the app. No point rewriting what already works. 

**d correct the button label, move the key steps higher up the page** (readers shouldn't have to scroll past intro paragraphs to find the actual instructions), and add the missing next step. 

A small before-and-after makes the difference tangible:

- Before: "Accede a tu perfil y completa la verificación."
- After: "En tu cuenta, selecciona 'Verificar cuenta' y sigue los pasos para subir el documento que se te solicita."

I'd only add a line about what appears after submission once the operator confirms what the player actually sees. Not before.

### What I'd hand back to your team

The deliverable, in this case, would be three things: 

- the revised Spanish copy
- a short note explaining what I checked and against what source
- a list of open questions the operator still needs to answer

**If I spotted the same outdated wording in the help centre or a canned support email, I'd flag those too**, so your team can decide whether to update them together rather than fixing one and leaving the others stale.

## What I'd Check After Updating the Page

Two questions.

### Are people finding it for the right searches?

I'd compare queries, impressions, and clicks for the page over two comparable periods; not just "before vs. after" in isolation, since seasonal shifts can distort that. 

If clicks change, I look at position and the mix of queries first before crediting the edit for the result.

### Are readers still getting stuck at the same step?

If the operator can share recent support feedback, **I'd look for repeat questions about verification, or about what happens after the document upload**. 

That tells me whether the fix actually worked, or whether the real issue sits with the app's wording or a support team's response.
